# Candidate optimization themes for Phase 2

Status: **candidates, not decided.** You asked for the themes to be broken down for the next phase. Each candidate lists the evidence in `papers/`, the part of the colleague's workflow it touches, the main risk, and a rough formulation. The evidence level is stated for each.

## What the 13 papers say about optimization (cross-cutting)

1. **Subgraph selection is mostly learned or heuristic, not solved as a combinatorial problem.** RareNet (note 01) learns edge scores by gradient descent; Policy-Learn (02) uses a Gumbel-softmax relaxation of discrete selection; One-shot (03) uses Personalized PageRank; KGTrimmer (04) learns a global mask. None of the 13 gives an exact or certified selection under a size or cost budget.
2. **Trade-offs are scalarized or hand-tuned.** RareNet's two-loss coupling (01), STAL's grid search (06), DynTKG's accuracy-vs-speed balance (07), and LinkedIn's multi-task weights (12) do not show a Pareto analysis in the parts read. The MOO survey (05) gives the vocabulary to make this explicit.
3. **Labeling budgets are a fixed count, not a cost.** STAL (06) queries a fixed number of nodes per round. There is no per-node labeling cost in the formulation. A cost-aware, budgeted selection is a gap, at least in these 13 papers.
4. **Ontology enters as structure or soft guidance, not as hard constraints.** KAT-GNN (08) adds SNOMED CT edges; OL-KGC (09) retrieves ontological knowledge for an LLM. The 01 draft's claim that ontology defines a hard feasible region is not yet supported by any of the 13 papers. This is an opportunity, with the caveat that the full texts are not all read.
5. **Rare-disease work is data-sparse and uses heuristics for weighting.** The DTI paper (10) reweights edges by a designed mechanism; RareNet (01) argues about evaluation protocols.

These are reading-level observations (abstracts, intros, and selected sections). Confirm each one in the full texts before it goes into the pre-project.

## A. Budgeted active learning for variant curation

**Question.** Given a budget of B expert curation or reclassification actions (e.g., VUS re-analysis, family follow-up), which variants or nodes should be labeled to maximize the model's accuracy or the number of reclassified P/LP variants?

- **Evidence:** STAL (06) is the baseline for label-efficient GNNs; Partition-Based AL and Batch AL with GNNs (from round 1 of `snowballing.md`) are the methodological candidates; Policy-Learn (02) provides the learned-policy alternative.
- **Colleague's workflow:** VUS classification and ACMG curation in the Genesis/Fleury flow (her doc, sections 5 and 9.1).
- **Rough formulation:** $max Σ_i g_i x_i  s.t.  Σ_i c_i x_i ≤ B, x_i ∈ {0,1}$, where $g_i$ is the expected information gain (or uncertainty) and $c_i$ the curation cost. With a coverage-type objective, the greedy algorithm has a (1 − 1/e) guarantee.
- **Main risk:** the cost $c_i$ and the gain $g_i$ depend on data the colleague may not have (curation time, prior probability of reclassification). Needs a pilot.
- **Evidence level:** medium-high for AL on graphs as a method; no paper in the set has the cost-aware formulation.

## B. Cascade testing as optimization

**Question.** For a proband with a P/LP variant, which relatives to test (given pedigree, cost and test availability) to maximize the expected number of carriers detected under a budget?

- **Evidence:** RareNet (01) and One-shot (03) provide subgraph-around-query ideas; the cascade-testing clinical papers from round 1 (JNCI 2023; cost-utility 2025) show the decision is recognized, but **no optimization-formulated cascade-testing paper was found** in one search round.
- **Colleague's workflow:** P2 in her doc (cascade testing structure, subgraphs of relatives, `testado_para` edges).
- **Rough formulation:** $max Σ_r p_r y_r  s.t.  Σ_r c_r y_r ≤ B$, with $p_r$ the probability of carrying the variant (from Mendelian inheritance and pedigree) and $c_r$ the cost. Integer programming on a small pedigree; extension to sequential testing as a Markov decision process.
- **Main risk:** data for family testing may be sparse in the Genesis/Fleury cohort; the carrier probability model needs validation.
- **Evidence level:** this is the most novel candidate in this list and is **untested** against the literature so far. Run a dedicated search before committing.

## C. Multi-objective, ontology-constrained subgraph/KG pruning

**Question.** Which subgraph (or pruned KG) should a GNN use, to maximize accuracy and ontological validity and minimize size, subject to hard constraints (evidence paths must stay, types must be valid)?

- **Evidence:** the current 01 draft (theme C in its own text). Supporting papers: KGTrimmer (04), Policy-Learn (02), One-shot (03), KAT-GNN (08); the MOO taxonomy (05) for the methods.
- **Colleague's workflow:** the KG schema and subgraph retrieval for GraphRAG and R-GCN (her sections 8 and 9.5).
- **Rough formulation:** $max (AUC(S), −|S|, −$violations$(S), $coverage$(S))$  s.t.  hard ontology constraints; exact on small KGs (ILP), NSGA-II or ε-constraint on larger ones.
- **Main risk:** this is the theme closest to existing work (KG pruning and sparsification are active in 2025–2026); the novelty claim needs a careful related-work check.
- **Evidence level:** high for the problem (many papers), medium for the ontology-constraint angle (no paper in the set enforces hard constraints formally).

## D. Multi-objective tuning of the R-GCN

**Question.** How should the R-GCN's hyperparameters (depth, basis count, dropout, learning rate, number of parameters) be chosen when AUC, calibration (ECE) and model size conflict?

- **Evidence:** the MOO survey (05) for Pareto and scalarization; One-shot (03) for Bayesian optimization with a random-forest surrogate; STAL (06) for the hyperparameter grid used in practice.
- **Colleague's workflow:** her node classification and link prediction (her section 9.5, metrics AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, F1).
- **Rough formulation:** multi-objective Bayesian optimization (e.g., ParEGO or multi-objective TPE) over the R-GCN configuration space, reporting the Pareto front of (AUC, ECE, parameters).
- **Main risk:** the least novel of the candidates; it is a well-known practice. Its value for the thesis is as a component that strengthens A–C, not as the main contribution.
- **Evidence level:** high for the method; low for novelty on its own.

## E. Explainability as constrained optimization

**Question.** What is the smallest subgraph that still supports a prediction (a minimal sufficient explanation), and how does it relate to the subgraph selection in C?

- **Evidence:** RareNet (01) retrieves patient subgraphs for interpretability; her proposal uses GNNExplainer (her section 9.5). No paper in the set formulates explanation as an exact constrained selection.
- **Rough formulation:** $min |S|  s.t.  f(S) ≥ τ  ($prediction preserved$)$, with ILP or a relaxation; compare to GNNExplainer's masked objective.
- **Main risk:** thinner evidence in the set; overlaps with C.
- **Evidence level:** low; keep it as a sub-question of C, not as a theme on its own, unless the snowballing finds more.
